  Samples are washed before we commit to a length. A cloth that only looks right before laundry does not survive a season, and the seams tell on it. If the stitch puckers, the weave is fighting the make.

  We keep the washed sample next to the fresh one so the settling is visible. Hems are planned for that settle, not for the cloth as it arrived.

  The floor test is the last one. If the garment swings away from the body or clings when you walk, the weight is wrong, however nice the swatch was.

The first cut of a season is a cloth decision. Colour comes after the weight feels right in the hand.

We crush a length, hold it to the window, and then watch it move on a person. Linen that is too fine wrinkles into strings. A wool that is too thick belongs in a coat. The edit keeps the weights you can wear from morning to the end of a work day.

A new colour only joins the rail if it can sit next to the coat, the shirt, and the trouser already there. Matching a trend is less useful than sharing a wash.
