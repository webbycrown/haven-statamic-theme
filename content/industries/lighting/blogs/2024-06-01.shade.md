side_copy: |
  Shape is a practical choice. A drum throws light up and down. A deeper shade throws it down, which is what a desk wants. A very wide shade looks generous and hits you in the eye when you stand up. Measure the shade against the seat, not against the catalogue.

  Paper is lighter and cheaper and it colours the room yellow by the second winter. Linen costs more because it stays nearer to the colour you bought.

A shade is how the bulb meets the room.

We use linen because it takes a warm bulb without turning the wall orange, and because it can be dusted without denting. Paper dents. Plastic shines. Linen sits between them and looks like cloth, which is what a room of wood and paper wants.

The shade is not a cover for a cheap glare. If the bulb is wrong, the shade will not save it. Fix the bulb first.
