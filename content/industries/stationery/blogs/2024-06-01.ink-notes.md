side_copy: |
  Dry time is part of the ink. A wet, beautiful line that smears when you turn the page is the wrong ink for a notebook you write in quickly. Blot if you are in a hurry. Do not close the book on a wet line and hope.

  Shake nothing. Sediment in some inks is normal. Rolling the bottle gently is enough. A hard shake fills the pen with bubbles and the line skips.

  If the ink is for a letter you will post, give it an hour. A warm envelope seals a damp address into a blur.
closing: |
  Dark, capped, dry before the book closes. The colour can be quiet. The line should still be there next year.

  A stained hand washes off. A stained page does not. Keep a scrap beside the bottle for the first stroke after filling, so the blob stays off the letter.

Ink notes are about the line you will want to read later.

We keep a dark ink in the edit because pale inks look delicate and fail the first time the page is read in a hurry. The bottle is glass so you can see the level and the colour without opening it. Fill the pen over a cloth. The linen wipe from a workshop would do. A paper towel leaves lint in the nib.

Store the bottle upright, away from the window. Sun changes a dye, and a bottle on its side is a desk you will not enjoy cleaning.
