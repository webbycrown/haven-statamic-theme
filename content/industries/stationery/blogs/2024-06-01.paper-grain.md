Paper grain is the quiet difference between a page you enjoy and a page you fight.

We look at sheets in the same low light you will write in, not under a bright bench lamp that hides the tooth. A page should show a soft grain and still let a sentence dry without a halo. If the ink spreads into the fibre, the sheet is too open for that pen. If the ink sits on top and smears an hour later, the sheet is too closed.

The notebooks in the edit are the ones that passed both tests with an ordinary dark ink.
