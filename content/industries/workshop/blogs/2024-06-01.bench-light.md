side_copy: |
  Clamp the lead so it does not cross the bench. A cord in the path of a chisel is how a lamp gets pulled onto the floor. The brass arm in this edit stays put once you set it. Tighten it before you start, not after it has drifted onto the work.

  Keep the shade above the splash of finish and glue. Paper and linen both stain, and a stained shade is a darker room next month.

  One lamp is enough for one pair of hands. A second light is for a second bench, not for removing every shadow. Some shadow is how you see a surface.
closing: |
  Left or right, in front of the shoulder, bright enough for a line. Then leave it there.

  The lamp is a tool. It does not need to be moved to look considered. The work moves under it.

Bench light is a position, not a brighter bulb.

We set the arm so the pool of light covers the hands and a little of the bench beyond them. The rest of the shop can stay dim. A floodlit room flattens the wood and you stop seeing tear-out until the shaving is already wrong.

If you are left-handed, reverse the rule. The lamp goes on the right. The principle is the same: the shadow leaves the cut.
